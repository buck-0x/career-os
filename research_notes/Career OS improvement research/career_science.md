# Career Development and Career-Counseling Science for career-os

Scope: validated frameworks, instruments and methods that career-os (ground-truth-interview, interest-radar, career-strategist) could adopt, with translation into interview questions or decision logic. Researched 2026-10-04 with a budget of about 15 tool calls; several primary sources were paywalled (noted in Gaps). Items marked "background knowledge, unverified this session" in Inferences come from my training data and were not re-checked against a source here; the report writer should treat them as lower confidence.

## 1. Career construction theory, the Career Construction Interview (CCI), narrative counseling and life design

### Takeaway
The CCI is a short, structured narrative interview (role models, favorite media, favorite story, motto, early recollections) that surfaces life themes. Its evidence is mostly small-sample, qualitative or quasi-experimental, though positive: clearer goals and gains in career adaptability that held at 3 months. It works well as a complement to career-os's checklist-style interview. Its companion construct, career adaptability, has a well-validated free scale (CAAS).

### Cited Findings
- CCI core prompts: role models (aspirational self and ideal identity), magazines/media (interests and preferred environments), favorite story (a script for the person's own transition), motto (self-advice and guiding principle), early recollections (the formative preoccupation). — [Savickas et al., My Career Story: Description and Initial Validity Evidence](https://www.marksavickas.com/files/1_Savickas_bio/Career%20Construction%20Theory/Publications/Articles/MCS_Validity.pdf)
- My Career Story (MCS) is a self-guided workbook version of the CCI; its validity article reports only "initial" evidence. My fetch could not extract sample sizes or coefficients. — [Savickas et al., MCS validity PDF](https://www.marksavickas.com/files/1_Savickas_bio/Career%20Construction%20Theory/Publications/Articles/MCS_Validity.pdf)
- After the CCI, an experimental group's Future Career Autobiographies showed more specific life and occupational goals than before. — [Effectiveness of the CCI from the perspective of life designing (South African journal)](https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/EJC132705); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305681429_The_effectiveness_of_the_career_construction_interview_from_the_perspective_of_life_designing)
- The CCI promoted students' career adaptability, and the gain was still present 3 months after the intervention. Three sessions are typical; most of the effect comes in the first few, and 4 or more sessions did no better than 3. — [IAFOR 2024, Career Construction Counseling: Exploring Intervention, Intensity](https://papers.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/papers/bce2024/BCE2024_83395.pdf)
- A shortened CCI form has been tested with high-school students who had career indecision. — [ERIC EJ1062932](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1062932.pdf)
- The Career Adapt-Abilities Scale (CAAS-International 2.0, Savickas & Porfeli 2012) has 24 items: 6 each for Concern, Control, Curiosity and Confidence, rated 1 ("not strong") to 5 ("very strong"). Total-score reliability is .92 (subscales .74 to .85). It was validated across 13 countries with configural and metric invariance. — [Savickas & Porfeli 2012 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262561417_Career_Adapt-Abilities_Scale_Construction_reliability_and_measurement_equivalence_across_13_countries); [CAAS-SF, Maggiori et al.](https://www.marksavickas.com/files/1_Savickas_bio/Interviews/Savickas%20Publications/Journal%20Articles/Maggoiori_Shortl.pdf)
- A 12-item short form (CAAS-SF, Maggiori, Rossier & Savickas) exists. — [CAAS-SF PDF](https://scispace.com/pdf/career-adapt-abilities-scale-short-form-caas-sf-construction-2k6tb110gz.pdf); [UNIL IRIS full version](https://iris.unil.ch/bitstreams/61c99db9-625f-49d8-b7e6-b0029e7df81e/download)

### Inferences
- **Add a "narrative themes" module to ground-truth-interview** using the five CCI prompts, adapted for adults:
  1. "Who did you admire growing up (not parents)? What about them?" Code the adjectives as the target self.
  2. "What media, channels, newsletters or subreddits do you consume by choice? What happens there?" This captures preferred environments and feeds interest-radar directly.
  3. "What is your favorite story right now (book, film, game)? Tell me the plot." This reveals the person's own transition script.
  4. "What is your favorite saying or motto?" This becomes the person's advice to themselves, which the strategist can quote back.
  5. "Give me three early memories." This reveals the preoccupation the person turns into an occupation.
  The agent then links the answers to state a life theme ("I want to move from X to Y by doing Z"). For hybrid users this offers a positioning sentence that a job title cannot give.
- Cap the narrative module at about 3 sessions or passes. Repeating it shows diminishing returns.
- **Use CAAS dimensions as a readiness check before strategy.** Low Concern (no future orientation), low Control (no ownership), low Curiosity (no exploration) or low Confidence (low self-efficacy) each calls for a different intervention. Low Curiosity, for example, calls for experiments before choosing.
- Background knowledge, unverified this session: the CCI often opens with "How can I be useful to you as you construct your career?" Savickas's 2011 book *Career Counseling* (APA) describes the full protocol.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the licensing of CAAS item text for commercial or embedded agent use. The items appear in open-access articles, and researchers widely use them without fees, but I found no explicit license statement. The report writer should flag this as "verify with the authors".
- I found no RCTs comparing the CCI with trait-factor counseling in adults mid-career. Most CCI evidence comes from student or small samples.
- Specific psychometrics for My Career Story (n, reliability) could not be extracted.

## 2. Ibarra (Working Identity), Designing Your Life, Newport (career capital): concrete practices

### Takeaway
All three reject "introspect first, then act". Ibarra and Burnett & Evans prescribe small real-world experiments, conversations and multiple parallel "possible selves". Newport prescribes building rare, valuable skills before demanding autonomy or passion. The evidence base is mostly qualitative (Ibarra studied 39 career changers, from background knowledge) or practitioner/program evaluation (DYL). No RCTs were found for these books' methods.

### Cited Findings
- Ibarra: career transition is "a crooked journey" of trying on "possible selves", not a linear move to a predetermined identity. There are three levers: (1) experiment with new professional activities ("crafting experiments"), (2) interact with new networks ("shifting connections"), (3) make sense of what is happening ("making sense", building a coherent story). Small wins sustain momentum. Knowing results from doing, not the reverse. — [Working Identity summary, careernlifedesign.com](https://careernlifedesign.com/working-identity/); [Internet Archive listing](https://archive.org/details/workingidentity00herm); [Google Books, updated edition](https://books.google.com/books/about/Working_Identity_Updated_Edition_With_a.html?id=Yh2rEAAAQBAJ)
- Burnett & Evans name "dysfunctional beliefs" such as "your degree determines your career", "if you're successful you'll be happy" and "you should know where you're going". — [Stanford eCorner clip](https://stvp.stanford.edu/clips/three-dysfunctional-beliefs/); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/advice/2018/01/29/ways-build-career-and-life-beyond-phd-opinion)
- Reported evaluation of Stanford's DYL course: students held fewer dysfunctional beliefs, generated more career ideas and options, and better conceived their futures. Reported gains also include career self-efficacy, novel ideation, career certainty and lower career anxiety. These come from secondary and program reporting, not peer-reviewed RCTs I could locate. — [Stanford News, 2017](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2017/06/spreading-life-design-thinking-universities); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/advice/2018/01/29/ways-build-career-and-life-beyond-phd-opinion)
- Newport: "follow your passion" is flawed; passion is an output of mastery. Desirable work traits (creativity, impact, control) are rare and valuable, so a person must trade rare and valuable skills ("career capital") for them, built through deliberate practice with a "craftsman mindset". — [Commoncog summary](https://commoncog.com/so-good-they-cant-ignore-you/); [Dean Yeong book notes](https://www.deanyeong.com/book-notes/so-good-they-cant-ignore-you)

### Inferences
- **Change the strategist's output from "pick a target" to "design experiments."** For each surviving option, emit 1 to 3 cheap tests: a prototype conversation with someone doing the work, a weekend or side project, or a paid micro-gig. Each test needs a hypothesis ("I expect to feel energized by X; evidence would be Y"). Log results back into the profile (Ibarra's crafting experiments plus DYL prototyping). This also fits the Levitt finding under question 4.
- **Odyssey Plans** (background knowledge, unverified this session): DYL asks for three distinct 5-year plans: (1) the current path, (2) what you would do if path 1 vanished, (3) what you would do if money and image did not matter. Each plan gets a six-word title, open questions, and gauges for resources, liking, confidence and coherence. career-os could generate three Odyssey plans from the profile and have the user rate each on these gauges. This counters premature single-option focus.
- **Good Time Journal** (background knowledge, unverified this session): log daily activities with an engagement rating and an energy rating, then reflect using AEIOU prompts (Activities, Environments, Interactions, Objects, Users). This is a behavioral, time-sampled replacement for the recall-based energizers/drainers question, and it can run as a lightweight daily or weekly check-in prompt.
- **Dysfunctional-belief detection:** the interviewer can flag and reframe statements like "I need to know my passion first" or "my title defines what I can apply for". This matters for hybrid users.
- **Newport as a counterweight to interest-radar:** the strategist should score each option on "career capital leverage", meaning how much existing rare and valuable skill it uses and builds, not just pull. Pull (interests) without proof (capital) should be a flag, not a recommendation.
- Ibarra's "shifting connections" suggests interest-radar should capture people as well as topics: who is in the network around each interest and how weak ties could be activated.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed controlled evaluation of DYL. The claims above are program-reported and should be flagged as weak or moderate evidence.
- I did not verify Ibarra's original sample details (background knowledge: about 39 people), nor any quantitative test of her model.
- Newport's thesis is argument and anecdote, not empirical research. Related peer-reviewed work on "fit" versus "develop" implicit theories of passion (Chen et al.; O'Keefe, Dweck & Walton 2018) was not checked this session.

## 3. Validated instruments: RIASEC / O*NET Interest Profiler, work values, VIA, CliftonStrengths, CAAS. Which are free and agent-usable?

### Takeaway
O*NET tools are the clearest fit. They are CC BY-ND for verbatim use, and an O*NET Tools Developer License permits modification and integration, with attribution and a validation requirement. VIA is free to take but restricts redistributing its items and scoring key. CliftonStrengths is proprietary and paid. CAAS item text is published in journals, but no license terms were found.

### Cited Findings
- O*NET Career Exploration Tools (Interest Profiler, plus the retired Work Importance Locator/Profiler and Ability Profiler) come under two licenses. CC BY-ND 4.0 covers verbatim redistribution with credit to O*NET and USDOL/ETA. The O*NET Tools Developer License allows developers to "modify, edit, extend, or create new tools", requires the same attribution plus the notice "USDOL/ETA has not approved, endorsed, or tested these modifications", and requires validating products that incorporate the tools. The O*NET Database has separate licensing. — [O*NET Tools Content License](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_tools.html)
- The Interest Profiler (RIASEC: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, Conventional) is free online via My Next Move. A free downloadable paper Short Form is available, and there is an IP web-services API. — [O*NET IP page](https://www.onetcenter.org/IP.html); [IP Manual](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/IP_Manual.pdf); [O*NET Interest Profiler Services](https://services.onetcenter.org/ip); [Short Form psychometrics](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383086300_ONETR_Interest_Profiler_Short_Form_Paper-_and-Pencil_Version_Evaluation_of_Self-_Scoring_and_Psychometric_Characteristics_The_National_Center_for_ONET_Development)
- VIA Survey: free for the public for personal insight, with no permission needed for non-commercial personal use. Researchers request permission; there is no cost, and "no reasonable request is rejected". The questions and scoring key "cannot be used or distributed outside of the research project or published". — [VIA FAQ](https://www.viacharacter.org/faq); [VIA use & interpretation guidelines](https://ww1.viacharacter.org/faq/permissions-and-guidelines-for-use-and-interpretation)
- CliftonStrengths: proprietary Gallup assessment, Top 5 at $24.99 and all 34 at $59.99, with no free official version. — [Gallup store](https://store.gallup.com/); [Wikipedia: CliftonStrengths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CliftonStrengths)
- CAAS: 24 items, good reliability and cross-national invariance (see question 1). — [Savickas & Porfeli 2012](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262561417_Career_Adapt-Abilities_Scale_Construction_reliability_and_measurement_equivalence_across_13_countries)
- One study found self-estimates of abilities track personality traits more than measured abilities, while strongly predicting professional interests. This means a self-rated skill inventory partly measures interest and self-concept. — [Personality and Individual Differences, 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886920300404)

### Inferences
- **RIASEC as a shared coordinate system.** career-os can infer a Holland code from interest-radar entries and from interview answers. The agent can map each energizer and trophy-case win to R/I/A/S/E/C. Under the Developer License it can administer the 30- or 60-item Interest Profiler and then link to O*NET occupations by code. For technical-creative hybrids the typical I+A (and often E) profile is itself a positioning device. O*NET's occupational data can supply "adjacent occupations" for people without a standard title.
- **Work values:** the O*NET Work Importance Locator/Profiler (retired, but still covered by the license) uses six values: Achievement, Independence, Recognition, Relationships, Support, Working Conditions (background knowledge, unverified this session). A card-sort version (rank 20 needs statements) is a validated substitute for free-text values questions and improves comparability between options.
- **Strengths:** prefer a VIA-style list as a vocabulary for open conversation, with the user optionally taking the free VIA survey and pasting results. Do not embed VIA items. Avoid CliftonStrengths except as user-supplied input.
- **CAAS** as an optional readiness module (see question 1). Verify licensing first.
- Any modified O*NET tool shipped in an open-source plugin must carry attribution and the non-endorsement notice, and the "validate" clause needs interpretation.

### Gaps
- I did not verify a current O*NET work-values instrument (the WIL/WIP are "retired"), nor the precise item counts of the current Interest Profiler forms.
- No license statement was found for CAAS.
- Peer-reviewed validity evidence for CliftonStrengths was not reviewed (Gallup technical reports exist, but independent validation is thinner; unverified).

## 4. Decision science for career choice: multi-attribute decisions, regret, affective forecasting, decision journals, premortems

### Takeaway
The best-supported career-specific decision model is Gati's PIC (Prescreening, In-depth exploration, Choice). It closely matches career-os's "filter by dealbreakers, then overlap" logic and adds rank-ordered sequential elimination, a 7±2 shortlist, and a sensitivity check. Affective-forecasting research says people overestimate how intense and lasting their feelings about outcomes will be. Levitt's randomized coin-flip study suggests people stuck on a change-vs-stay decision are on average better off making the change. Premortems are popular, but their foundational "30%" claim measured the number of reasons generated, not their accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- PIC model (Gati & Asher, 2001): (1) Prescreening uses sequential elimination by aspects. Start with the most important aspect, eliminate incompatible options, then continue through aspects in rank order until you have a short list of about 7±2. (2) In-depth exploration of the shortlist. (3) Choice. — [Gati & Asher, Prescreening, In-Depth Exploration, and Choice](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/264415740_Prescreening_In-Depth_Exploration_and_Choice_From_Decision_Theory_to_Career_Counseling_Practice); [Sam Young summary](https://www.samyoung.co.nz/2026/08/gati-and-ashers-pic-framework.html)
- People who used sequential elimination and chose a recommended occupation reported greater satisfaction with their choice six years later. — [Gati & Asher via ResearchGate/Academia](https://www.academia.edu/23144198/Prescreening_In_Depth_Exploration_and_Choice_From_Decision_Theory_to_Career_Counseling_Practice)
- Gati's "Making Better Career Decisions" (MBCD) is an internet-based system implementing PIC. — [MBCD information for professionals](http://mbcd.intocareers.org/materials/MBCD_Information_for_Professionals.htm); [Helping Clients Make Better Career Decisions, CJCD](https://cjcd-rcdc.ceric.ca/index.php/cjcd/article/download/3272/2901/12319)
- Recent JVB work frames career decisions as "fast and slow" (intuitive and deliberative) and calls for integrative interventions. — [Career decision making, fast and slow (JVB 2020)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001879120300737); [Making better career decisions: From challenges to opportunities (JVB 2021)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001879121000178)
- Affective forecasting: people show an impact bias, overestimating the intensity and duration of emotional reactions. The drivers are focalism (ignoring the other things that will also affect feelings) and failing to anticipate their own sense-making, which speeds recovery. — [Wilson & Gilbert 2005, Current Directions](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.0963-7214.2005.00355.x); [Wilson & Gilbert 2013, "The Impact Bias Is Alive and Well"](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/WIlson_Gilbert_2013.pdf)
- Levitt (2021, Review of Economic Studies): in a large randomized field experiment, people undecided on important decisions such as quitting a job were assigned by coin toss. Those told to make a change were more likely to change and were happier six months later than those told to keep the status quo. This suggests excess caution about life changes. — [Levitt 2021, RES](https://academic.oup.com/restud/article-abstract/88/1/378/5834495); [NBER w22487](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22487)
- Premortem: Mitchell, Russo & Pennington (1989) found that imagining an outcome as certain increased the number of reasons generated by about 30%, but "did not assess the quality of the reasons". Klein's 2007 HBR framing ("increases the ability to correctly identify reasons") overstates this. Later studies (Veinott et al. 2010; Gallop & Bischoff 2016) give some further support. — [Jason Collins, "The premortem"](https://corporate.jcx.au/premortem); [Klein, HBR 2007 (PDF copy)](https://lmscontent.embanet.com/USC/PPD554/Week10/PPD554_W10_HBR_Klein_Performaing_a_Project_Premortem.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Change the strategist's filter to explicit PIC.**
  1. Ask the user to rank-order aspects (dealbreakers first, then the most important preferences), each with an acceptable range and a "compromise willingness" level. Gati's model distinguishes optimal and acceptable levels.
  2. Eliminate options sequentially and show which aspect killed each option, so the user can challenge it.
  3. Stop at about 5 to 9 options.
  4. Run a sensitivity check: "if you relaxed aspect X, these options return."
  5. Only then compute the pull×proof overlap.
  This mirrors the existing design but makes it transparent and evidence-backed.
- **Forecast-debiasing prompts.** When the user says "I'd hate X" or "Y would make me so happy", ask: (a) what else would fill a typical Tuesday in that role (counters focalism); (b) for a past change, how long did the feeling last; (c) what someone currently doing it reports. Surrogation (asking people in the role) beats simulation in Gilbert's work (background knowledge, unverified this session), which supports prototype conversations.
- **Status-quo bias nudge.** If the user is stuck between staying and changing, the strategist can cite Levitt's finding cautiously: it is one study, self-selected volunteers, and an average effect.
- **Decision journal:** at each strategy decision, record the options considered, the chosen option, the expected outcome with confidence, key assumptions, and a review date. Review later against outcomes. This creates a feedback loop the profile currently lacks. (No career-specific empirical evidence was found; this practice comes from general judgment literature.)
- **Premortem per top option:** "It's 18 months from now and this move failed; why?" Treat the output as risk brainstorming only; do not claim it improves accuracy.
- **Regret minimization** (Bezos-style "at 80, will I regret not trying?"): I found no direct empirical validation this session. Present it as a heuristic, not evidence-based.

### Gaps
- No empirical evidence was checked on whether decision journals improve career outcomes.
- Regret-minimization framing was not verified empirically. Separate literature (Gilovich & Medvec, on regretting inactions more over the long term) is relevant but was not checked.
- Formal MCDM/MAUT (weighted additive scoring) for careers: no head-to-head evidence against PIC was found.

## 5. Self-assessment accuracy and better data elicitation (behavioral event interviews, critical incidents, 360 feedback, Reflected Best Self)

### Takeaway
Self-ratings of ability correlate only about .29 with objective performance. Accuracy improves when ratings are domain-specific, task-objective and familiar, when people expect verification, and when they make explicit social comparisons. career-os's "hard skills self-rated" field is therefore its weakest data. It should be anchored with behavioral evidence (CAR stories, already present) and with others' input, such as the Reflected Best Self Exercise.

### Cited Findings
- Mabe & West (1982), 55 studies: mean correlation between self-evaluation of ability and performance r = .29 (SD = .25). Accuracy rose when people expected their self-assessment to be validated, were told to compare themselves with others, had experience self-evaluating, and were assured anonymity. — [Mabe & West 1982 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232439390_Validity_of_self-evaluation_of_ability_A_review_and_meta-analysis)
- Zell & Krizan (2014), a synthesis of 22 meta-analyses: mean self-evaluation and performance correlation was .29 (range .09 to .63). It was stronger for domain-specific rather than broad ratings, and for objective, familiar or low-complexity tasks. — [Zell & Krizan 2014, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691613518075)
- Self-estimates of abilities reflect personality more than ability and strongly predict interests. — [Personality and Individual Differences 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886920300404)
- There is a meta-analytic process model of self-other agreement in job performance ratings. — [Self-Other Agreement in Job Performance Ratings (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24187174_Self-Other_Agreement_in_Job_Performance_Ratings_A_Meta-Analytic_Test_of_a_Process_Model)
- Reflected Best Self Exercise (Roberts, Dutton, Spreitzer, Heaphy & Quinn, AMR 2005): collect short behavioral stories of when you were at your best from 10 to 20 people across work, friends and family; find patterns; write a "best-self portrait". It was theorized to boost energy, self-development and self-aligned goals. — [Roberts et al. 2005 AMR PDF](https://www.unm.edu/~plutgen/Best-self%20Feedback%20Process%20Heaphy%20et%20al%202005.pdf); [Michigan Ross Center for Positive Organizations, RBSE](https://positiveorgs.bus.umich.edu/cpo-tools/rbse/)
- Field experiment with adolescent leaders: examined the psychological resources linked to the source and valence of best-self feedback. — [Spreitzer et al., RBSE field experiment](https://webuser.bus.umich.edu/spreitze/Pdfs/BestSelfRevision.pdf)
- Including the RBSE in management curricula increased students' interview self-efficacy. — [AMLE, Looking in the Mirror](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/amle.2021.0347)
- The RBSE is trademarked ("Reflected Best Self Exercise™") and distributed by the Center for Positive Organizations, where the full kit appears to be a paid product. — [CPO RBSE page](https://positiveorgs.bus.umich.edu/cpo-tools/rbse/)

### Inferences
- **Change "hard skills self-rated".**
  - Make each rating domain-specific and anchored: "Which of these could you do unaided today: [concrete task ladder]?" rather than 1 to 5 on "Python".
  - Require one piece of evidence per rating above "basic", linked to a trophy-case entry or an artifact URL.
  - Ask a social-comparison question: "Compared with the people you've worked with on this, what percentile?"
  - Tell the user the rating will be checked against evidence. Mabe & West found that expected validation improves accuracy.
  - Store a confidence-weighted score: self-rating × evidence strength.
- **Behavioral event interview / critical incident technique** (background knowledge, unverified this session: McClelland's BEI from competency modeling; Flanagan 1954 CIT). The trophy-case CAR format already approximates this. Strengthen it with neutral probes ("what exactly did you do, say, think next?"), require "I" not "we", collect a failure or difficult incident as well as wins, and code competencies from the actions rather than from self-labels. Code "anti-skills" from incidents too, not only from self-report.
- **Reflected best self as a "proof from others" input.** Generate a short message the user sends to 5 to 10 contacts: "Tell me a specific time I was at my best and what I did." The agent then codes the themes into strengths. Because the RBSE name and kit are trademarked and paid, implement a generic "others' best-self stories" prompt with attribution to the 2005 AMR article rather than reproducing the kit.
- **Lightweight 360:** ask the user to paste performance reviews, peer feedback or LinkedIn recommendations, then mine them for recurring strengths and blind spots. Self-other gaps should populate the existing "blind spots" field with evidence instead of self-report.
- **Strategist logic:** weight evidence-backed skills (other-reported or artifact-backed) above self-rated ones when computing "proof".

### Gaps
- No quantitative outcome evidence for the RBSE on career decisions was found; outcomes are mostly self-efficacy and qualitative.
- The Dunning-Kruger pattern specifically was not checked this session.
- 360-feedback validity for career-choice purposes was not reviewed.

## 6. Portfolio careers, multipotentialites, T-shaped / comb-shaped / hybrid professionals: positioning

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence comes from sociology of labor markets, not career counseling. Audiences penalize category-spanning ("generalist discount"). The penalty shrinks when the person is clearly relevant to the specific job, has a strong reputation, specializes on one dimension (such as function) while spanning another (such as industry), and presents a coherent ordering of experiences. "Multipotentialite" and "T-shaped" are practitioner terms with little peer-reviewed validation.

### Cited Findings
- Actors who span category distinctions are hard for audiences to understand and tend to be ignored or devalued (the generalist or hybrid penalty). — [Leung, "An idiosyncrasy credit or a generalist discount?", Berkeley IRLE](https://irle.berkeley.edu/publications/working-papers/an-idiosyncrasy-credit-or-a-generalist-discount-conditional-advantages-to-working-broadly-in-a-virtual-labor-market/)
- On an online freelance market (Elance), spanning was rewarded when applicants showed relevance to the job, and for highly reputable applicants, whose breadth reads as superior skill. — [Leung, IRLE working paper](https://irle.berkeley.edu/publications/working-papers/an-idiosyncrasy-credit-or-a-generalist-discount-conditional-advantages-to-working-broadly-in-a-virtual-labor-market/)
- Leung (2014, American Sociological Review), "Dilettante or Renaissance Person? How the Order of Job Experiences Affects Hiring": the sequence and coherence of varied experiences affects whether employers read breadth as dilettantism or versatility. I could not fetch the full text (HTTP 403); the title and venue are confirmed. — [Leung 2014, ASR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0003122413518638)
- Founders who are generalists on one dimension (industry) but specialists on another (function) face less devaluation at IPO fundraising. — [Specialists, Generalists, or Both? Organization Science 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ororsc/v34y2023i2p557-588.html)
- Kacperczyk & Younkin, "The paradox of breadth", is further work on category spanning and its tradeoffs. Its findings were not fetched. — [LBS Research Online](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/786/1/Kacperczyk%20Paradox%20of%20breadth.pdf)
- Lateral moves inside organizations can lead to career advancement ("stepping sideways to step up"). — [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2021.03746)

### Inferences
- **Positioning rules for career-os's hybrid users:**
  1. **Lead with one legible specialty per audience.** Span on the other dimension, for example "design engineer for developer tools" (specialist in function, breadth in domain). This is the "specialist on one axis" finding.
  2. **Relevance first, breadth second.** For each target, the strategist should select the 2 or 3 trophy-case wins most relevant to that audience and present the other skills as differentiators, not as the headline. This is the Elance finding.
  3. **Narrate the order.** Present varied experience as a coherent arc using the CCI life theme, so it reads as "renaissance" rather than "dilettante" (Leung 2014).
  4. **Build reputation signals** (public artifacts, testimonials, recognized outputs). Reputation converts spanning from a penalty into a premium.
- Add a strategist output: "category the audience will put you in" plus "spanning-penalty risk", per target.
- "T-shaped", "comb-shaped" and "multipotentialite" (Emilie Wapnick) can serve as user-facing vocabulary but should not be presented as validated constructs.

## Cross-cutting: what makes career interventions work (applies to all modules)

These findings span all questions and are recorded here for the report writer. They inform how the agent should behave.

- Brown & Ryan Krane (2000) and Brown et al. (2003), 62 studies: five "critical ingredients" each raise career-choice intervention effects: (1) written exercises or workbooks, (2) individualized interpretation and feedback, (3) world-of-work information, (4) modeling, (5) attention to building support. Interventions with none of them averaged an effect size of .22. — [Brown et al. 2003, JVB 62:411-428](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001879102000520)
- Whiston et al. (2017) replication, 57 studies (1996 to 2015, n = 7,364): added counselor support, values clarification and psychoeducation as critical components. Career decision self-efficacy outcomes showed the largest average effect, d ≈ .45. — [Whiston et al. 2017, JVB 100:175-184](https://ohiostate.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/effectiveness-of-career-choice-interventions-a-meta-analytic-repl/); [summary via Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Effectiveness-of-career-choice-interventions:-A-and-Whiston-Li/3f953b10c3fd71629b9aaee9178911dd90c48c3a)
- Individual career counseling produces moderate-to-large pre-post change (d from .41 to 1.08). — [Meta-analysis of individual career counseling, Journal of Employment Counseling](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/joec.12239)
- Inference: career-os already covers written exercises, individualized feedback and values clarification. It most lacks **world-of-work information** (use O*NET data and real job postings), **modeling** (stories of people who made similar hybrid moves; prototype conversations) and **building support** (who in the user's network will back this; Ibarra's shifting connections). An AI agent can partly supply "counselor support" through warmth and continuity, but Whiston's finding concerns human counselors. Whether an AI counts is untested and should be flagged.
