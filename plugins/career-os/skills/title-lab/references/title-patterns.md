# Title Patterns for Non-Standard Careers

Use these to generate candidates. Each pattern trades findability against nuance; good title stacks usually combine a findable anchor with an expressive pattern.

| Pattern | Shape | Examples | Strengths | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard anchor | Common market title | Product Designer, Staff Engineer, Product Manager | Searchable, comp is legible | Flattens hybrid skills; may imply anti-skills |
| Anchor + modifier | Modifier + standard title | Design Engineer, Technical Product Designer, Creative Technologist | Searchable and differentiated | Modifier may be unfamiliar to some recruiters |
| Domain-qualified | Title, Domain | Product Lead, Climate Tech; Staff Engineer, AI Infrastructure | Signals focus | Narrows the funnel |
| Compound / slash | A + B or A / B | Engineer + Designer; Strategist / Builder | Honest about hybrids | Can read as unfocused; algorithms parse badly |
| Function-over-form | What you do, not the role name | Builds AI-native operating models; Turns research into products | Memorable, accurate | Not searchable; use as positioning, not anchor |
| Scope signal | Founding / Principal / Head of / Fractional | Founding Engineer, Fractional CTO, Principal Designer | Signals seniority and stage fit | Seniority mismatch closes doors either way |
| Emerging title | Newer titles gaining adoption | AI Engineer, Forward Deployed Engineer, Design Technologist, Developer Experience Lead | Rides demand | Meanings vary widely; verify with postings |
| Invented + explained | Novel title + one-line gloss | "Systems Choreographer: I design how teams, tools, and AI work together" | Distinctive, conversational | Category creation: never as anchor; needs the gloss every time |

## Where emerging hybrid titles anchor (as of 2026; verify with live postings)

| Title | Market it anchors in | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Design Engineer | Design-led product companies (developer tools, SaaS) | Established at companies like Vercel and Linear; no reliable posting-volume data |
| Creative Technologist | Advertising, agencies, brand and experiential studios | Umbrella title; meaning varies widely between shops |
| Forward Deployed Engineer | AI and data platform companies selling to enterprises | Fastest-growing of the group: Indeed postings rose from 643 (Apr 2025) to 5,330 (Apr 2026) |
| AI Engineer | Broad; increasingly outside tech | US titles with AI terms tripled 2022→2026 and 63% are outside tech, so "AI" alone signals less each year |

O*NET maps most of these to broad codes (e.g., "design engineer" returns mechanical and aerospace engineering first), which is exactly why the anchor needs an OR-cluster partner and posting research.

## Generation heuristics

- Start from the portfolio **themes** and ground-truth **energizers**, not from org-chart titles.
- Strip any word implying an **anti-skill** (e.g., "Manager" if they don't want to manage people).
- Test seniority both ways: generate one title a level up and one a level down, and check reactions.
- Every expressive title needs an anchor partner it can sit beside.
- Prefer an existing category for the anchor; treat any invented title as category creation (see SKILL.md, step 5).
- Hybrids pay a "generalist discount" with readers unless they look like a specialist on one axis: pick the one function the title asserts (engineer, designer, strategist) and let the modifier or domain carry the other axis.
- Verify emerging titles against real postings; their meaning drifts fast.
- Read titles out loud in a sentence: "I'm a ___ at ___." Awkward phrasing gets dropped.

## Reaction vocabulary

Help users articulate reactions with these dimensions:
- **Signal:** builder vs. advisor, craftsperson vs. leader, specialist vs. generalist
- **Audience:** who would reach out because of this title (and who would be filtered out)
- **Seniority:** too junior, right, overreaching
- **Scope:** too narrow, right, too vague
- **Tone:** credible, playful, hype-y, corporate, academic
- **Work implied:** does it promise the work they want more of?
